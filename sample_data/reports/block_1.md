# Staff Report — Block 1 (sessions 1-4, Feb 10 to Feb 21, 2026)

## Coach — Technical

The serve is mechanically fine at the start of the block and drifts over four sessions: untracked baseline (S1), three double faults in a doubles match (S2), four in a practice serving set (S3), five across a three-set singles loss (S4). She reports the serve as "solid" or "fine" in the same entries. There is no mechanical failure visible in her own descriptions; what is visible is a trend she is not naming.

Backhand approach volley is the technical gap she does name: footwork late (S1), setup late off Rhea's returns (S2). It shows up in her voice as an actual complaint, unlike the serve. Trust her on this one.

Do not treat the serve trend as a mechanics issue yet. The data is thin and the setups vary (practice vs. match, first-set vs. third). Watch it for one more block. If double faults keep drifting up in matches specifically, hand it off.

**This block:** ten minutes of backhand approach-volley footwork drill at the start of every practice session.

## Sport Psychologist — Mental

Entry state is uneven across the block and correlates with what happens on court. Session 2 she arrived "scattered, rushed from a call" and made three double faults in her serve games. Session 4 she arrived "focused, warmed up properly" and still lost the third set on serve. The pattern to watch is not "bad state equals bad tennis" — it is that she under-prepares mentally for matches while over-preparing for practice.

Third-set nerves showed in S4 as a serve issue in her own account. Whether it is the serve or the arriving-under-pressure is not visible yet.

Four sessions is not enough to name a real pattern. This is a note for next block.

**This block:** three minutes of unstructured breathing before every match, at the court. Not a routine, a floor.

## Tactics Analyst — Strategy

The reactive default is the finding. In S4 she wrote it plainly: "Priya kept moving me side to side and I kept sending the ball right back to where she was, especially on the run. She had the middle of the court and I never made her cover the whole thing." That is not a shot-selection error, it is a court-reading error. Under time pressure she plays the ball back to the person feeding it.

In doubles (S2) she left poaching opportunities on the table when Rhea served well. Same underlying pattern: she waits to be moved rather than moving the opponent.

The reactive default is visible in both singles and doubles. It is the single most valuable thing to work on this block; a technical drill for it does not exist, only match reps with the pattern named.

**This block:** in every match point, ask before hitting: "Where is she standing? Hit away from that." Say it out loud between points if needed.
